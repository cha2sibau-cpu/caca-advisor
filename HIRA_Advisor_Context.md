## 01 · Who I am & what HIRA is

**Arza "Caca" Nursatya** — employee and head of creative department at HIRA Imaji, Jakarta. In-house photographer turned creative leader. Joined HIRA in 2017 as the first to graduate from the company's apprenticeship program. Currently the commercial revenue anchor of the photographer roster.

**HIRA Imaji** is a photography-based advertising production company. The medium is photography — that's the only one. But the identity is really about production excellence across every dimension: from the first briefing to the final deliverable. Photography is not a constraint; it's the craft context inside which everything else has to be equally good.

That means: the room smells right, the food on set is good, the Wi-Fi is reliable, the art team is ready, the glam team is on time, the producer has thought through contingencies, the client service is warm and clear. Great photography surrounded by a sloppy production is still a failure. HIRA's signature is that everyone — photographer, assistant, producer, client service, art team, glam, security, cleaner — is in full work mode on the same day. That holistic standard is what HIRA protects and tries to elevate.

On AI and compositing: HIRA uses whatever the client needs and whatever serves the production. Real setups are the default because on production day, everything is visible to everyone and real is often the cleanest approach. But heavy compositing is equally valid — Arza has a background as a digital imaging artist and uses it regularly. The medium decision follows the brief, not an ideology.

HIRA operates across commercial lifestyle, kids, beauty/skincare, still life, food, and edgy/contemporary niches. Clients come through agencies and direct.

## 02 · HIRA's current strategic direction

HIRA is navigating an industry under AI disruption. The strategic position is not "photography vs. AI" — it's production excellence as the differentiator. The bet is that clients who care about quality (of output, of process, of experience) will pay for it. AI is a tool in that service, not a threat to be defended against.

Key differentiators to develop and protect:
- Holistic production standard (every role, every detail, every day)
- An apprenticeship pipeline that builds photographers from within
- A diverse roster that covers multiple niches with distinct voices
- Compositing capability (Arza's DI background) as a production asset

**The positioning problem to solve:** HIRA needs to be seen as the premium safe option *and* the creatively interesting option — not just one or the other. Currently, the roster skews toward "safe commercial" with isolated pockets of edge (Yogo, Vony). Closing that gap is a medium-term strategic priority.

**Competitive context:** Indonesian advertising production is crowded. Agencies are cost-pressured. AI is being used by some clients to bypass production entirely. HIRA's response must be value-based, not price-based.

**Last active pitch:** Smartfren "Aman" / #DiSiniDiSanaAman campaign through agency Salvo. HIRA positioned as photography-first production partner. Status: pending.

**Ongoing internal work:**
- Apprenticeship program management (particularly Yusef's stalled progression)
- Roster positioning and portfolio gap identification (Ester's portfolio build)
- HIRA's strategic repositioning in the face of AI disruption

**Current diagnosis on visibility:** HIRA has a perception problem, not a capability problem. From the outside, HIRA reads as expensive, intimidating, and only suitable for difficult/complex jobs — three barriers before a new client makes first contact. As Arza put it: *"From outside we're like snob kids that are unreachable and scary. People have to work with us first to know that assumption is wrong."* The Japanese craftsman culture (silent, serious, let the work speak) works at a certain level of fame. HIRA is not yet at that level, so the culture costs visibility without delivering the prestige payoff. An open question remains: HIRA has no systematic way of collecting outside perspective — most market intelligence comes from reading the room on pitches and word of mouth.

**Pond size problem:** The total addressable market is smaller than it looks. Big agencies in Jakarta number fewer than 20. Vendor rotation is partly political (agencies rotating to avoid nepotism accusations), not purely merit-based. HIRA currently has almost no presence in smaller agencies, PR agencies, or brand teams outside of existing relationships. This absence is not a deliberate choice — it has simply never been prioritized, and has calcified into a comfort zone around the existing agency pipeline. That pipeline is fragile, and Arza knows it.

**On alternative income streams:** Workshops and prewedding have been discussed at middle management level as potential new income streams. HIRA has R&D budget capacity (currently used to subsidize next-gen photographer pitches below cost, as market-buying). The instinct to explore alternatives is sound. The risk is using them as displacement activity when the core pond feels hard, rather than genuine strategic expansion. This needs a decision at leadership level — not just middle management conversation.

**Organizational structure note:** Directly above Arza is Edna, shadow COO, former agency account manager (2010–2018). Her mental model of the industry is dated — she still operates in "working hours" logic in an industry that moved away from that in the 90s. She is a structural friction point for new initiatives. Adi Prawira is the actual COO. Adhitya (CEO) will support new moves as long as there is proof of profit — he needs evidence before committing, not ideas.
